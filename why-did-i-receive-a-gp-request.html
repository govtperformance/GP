Why did I receive a GP request?

Why a Government Performance request matters and why a response is important.

Government Performance operates as a civic media and communications platform supporting communities seeking visibility around specific local government issues and actions. When GP reaches out, it is because an issue raised by members of the affected community has reached a level where information, context, or response from the relevant office, organization, or representative is important.

What GP is

A serious civic infrastructure and communications platform focused on local government issues communities want addressed.

Why a request is sent

To obtain relevant context, factual response, or clarification tied to a specific issue raised by the community you serve.

Why a response matters

It helps ensure that community-facing awareness materials include the most accurate and current information available.

How GP uses responses

Responses may appear in community visibility materials.

Responses may be used in reports, summary content, issue timelines, social media publications, video content, survey materials, or other awareness tools related to the issue at hand. GP’s goal is not disruption for its own sake. Its goal is to support communities seeking understanding, visibility, and accountability regarding matters affecting them.

If no response is received

Non-response is handled factually, not theatrically.

If a response is not received, GP may note that no response was provided regarding an issue raised by members of the affected community. That statement is intended to be factual and informative. It helps the public understand whether an office, organization, or representative responded to a matter that community members considered important enough to raise.

What to do next

How recipients should handle a GP request.

The strongest response is clear, timely, factual, and tied to the specific community issue identified in the request.

Acknowledge the issue

Confirm receipt and address the matter raised rather than relying on generic statements.

Provide relevant facts

Offer context, explanation, timelines, policy references, or next steps tied to the issue.

Respond with community awareness in mind

Remember that the issue originated with citizens in the community your office or organization supports.
